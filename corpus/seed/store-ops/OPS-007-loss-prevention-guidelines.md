# Loss Prevention Guidelines

Confidential. Describes detection methods and thresholds. Do not post in the break room or discuss on the sales floor.

## The rule that matters most

**Do not pursue, detain, or physically intervene.** No merchandise is worth an associate's safety or a wrongful detention claim. Observe, document, and report. This applies regardless of the value involved and regardless of how certain you are.

## Detection

Associates greet every customer within 30 seconds of entry. This is a service standard and the single most effective deterrent in the store.

High-shrink categories sit in sightlines from the register: optics, headlamps, electronics, and climbing hardware. Do not rearrange these into blind corners for a merchandising display without regional approval.

## Documented signals

Repeated visits without purchase followed by a large-bag entry. Requests to see multiple high-value items at once near close. Two customers separating so one draws staff attention while the other is unobserved. Tags found discarded in fitting rooms or restrooms.

None of these signals alone is evidence of anything. Several together warrant documentation, not confrontation.

## Internal shrink

Internal loss exceeds external loss in this category set. Signals include register variances clustering on one associate's shifts, refunds without matching sales, and voided transactions above the store average.

The cash handling separation rule exists for this reason: no associate both counts a drawer and approves its variance.

## Reporting

Report to the store manager and loss prevention the same day. Include time, description, and transaction numbers. Do not include a customer's name, photograph, or any characteristic that identifies a person by appearance.
