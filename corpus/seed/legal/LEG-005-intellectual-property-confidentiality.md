# Intellectual Property and Confidentiality

## Company information

Confidential information includes anything not publicly released: product plans, supplier terms, financial results before announcement, customer data, store performance, and source code. It stays confidential after you leave, with no expiration.

Financial results are confidential until publicly reported. Trading on or sharing unreleased results is a securities matter, not just a policy matter.

## Work you create

Work created within the scope of employment belongs to Kestrel Ridge. Work you create entirely on your own time, without company equipment or confidential information, and unrelated to company business, is yours.

If you are unsure which side something falls on, ask before publishing. Asking costs nothing; the alternative can cost the project.

## Open source

Contributing to open source in your own time is encouraged. Contributing company code requires approval through the open source intake, which checks license compatibility and whether the code embeds anything confidential.

Using open source in company products requires a license review. Copyleft licenses are permitted for internal tooling and require General Counsel approval for anything distributed or embedded in a customer-facing product.

## Other people's IP

Do not bring code, documents, or trade secrets from a previous employer. Do not use competitor materials marked confidential, however they reached you. Report it to Legal if you receive something you should not have.

Brand assets, photography, and outdoor imagery must be licensed for the use. A license for social media does not cover print or in-store display.
